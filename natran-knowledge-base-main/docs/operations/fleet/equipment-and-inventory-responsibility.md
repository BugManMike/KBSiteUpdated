Sections 2.1, 2.2 and 2.5 of the signed Employee Agreement, which cover the same ground from three
angles.

# The principle

The stated reasoning: when policies and procedures are followed exactly, accidents resulting in loss
are minimised. **So when a loss is directly attributable to a failure to follow policy, or a policy
violation, the cost is shared by the employee who caused it.**

**Normal wear and tear is distinguished from negligence.** Wear and tear is expected; negligent loss
or damage is charged.

# The standing authorisation

By signing the agreement, **the employee authorises the company to deduct from their paycheck** for
the reasons in the policy.

- The company **provides an itemised list of all equipment issued** to the employee, **with values**.
- Anything **lost, damaged through negligence, or not returned** on resignation or termination is
  **deducted from their paycheck**.
- **On separation, unreturned equipment is deducted from the final paycheck.**
- Replacement costs are as listed on the **payroll deduction authorization form**.

> **This sits awkwardly against the handbook.**
> [Employment classification](/people/employment-classification.md) states that employees "must be
> made aware of and consent to any deductions **before they are implemented**" and that "written
> documentation of such consent is required." This agreement is a blanket up-front consent. Whether
> signing it satisfies the handbook's per-deduction requirement, or whether a separate form is needed
> each time, is not resolved by either document. **HR to confirm** — it determines whether a
> deduction can be made without a fresh signature.

# Equipment loss

The employee is responsible for equipment, tools and supplies in their possession, and for
**company supplies, vehicles and workstations before, during and after work**.

- **Lost or misplaced items: replacement cost deducted from pay.**
- **Wear and tear:** turn the item in for replacement. **Disposing of it without management
  permission is treated as a loss** and charged to whoever disposed of it.
- **Losses must be reported to management immediately.**
- All losses accounted for **at market price at the time of replacement**, reviewed case by case.

> **On witnessing theft:** *"If you witness theft, you must report it. Failure to do so will make you
> an accomplice to the crime."* Reports **can be made anonymously**, and the reporter **is
> protected**.

# Vehicle and inventory

Each employee is **solely responsible for the vehicle and equipment assigned to them** and for the
items on **their inventory sheet** — including anything lost, stolen or broken through lack of care.
The company may **replace tools not returned at the completion of a job and deduct the cost**.

**Purchasing rules:**

- All purchases must follow **company purchasing procedures**.
- Goods must be **received and stored at company locations or in company vehicles**.
- **Failure to submit receipts or assign purchase order numbers results in a deduction of costs from
  pay.**

> **Storing pest control products or application equipment at home is strictly prohibited,
> "considered theft and will be prosecuted to the full extent of the law."** That is the strongest
> language in the agreement. It is a regulatory matter as much as a property one — licensed pesticide
> storage is controlled — though the agreement frames it purely as theft.

# The dollar figures elsewhere

Specific deduction amounts live in the vehicle policy rather than here: **$125** vehicle cleaning,
**$1,000** at-fault accident damage, **$1,000** flood or insecure-storage damage. See
[vehicle use policy](/operations/fleet/vehicle-use-policy.md). Uniforms are covered in
[uniform and dress code](/people/uniform-and-dress-code.md), and the **$45** check cancellation fee
in [employment classification](/people/employment-classification.md).

> No equivalent figure or cap exists for equipment — it is "market price at the time of replacement".
> The itemised issue list is therefore the only record of what an employee is exposed to.
