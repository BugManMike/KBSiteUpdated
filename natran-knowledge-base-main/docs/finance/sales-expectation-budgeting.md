# The shape of the exercise

1. **Determine each department's sales expectations and capacity**, and ask whether the quotas are
   realistic. Add cost-per-lead. Decide whether any of the expectation comes from creative or other
   sources.
2. **Adjust the ok / better / best cases to match.** *Ok should be about equal to the sales
   expectation* — so the base case is the plan, not the optimistic case.

# Step 1. Salesperson capacity per department

1. Locate each salesperson.
2. **Confirm every active salesperson is listed.**
3. Enter a sales expectation for each.

# Step 2. Sales expectation

Enter, per department:

- average expected **initial value**
- average expected **monthly invoice value**
- expected **% of recurring customers**
- expected **lead closure rate**
- expected **percentage of new customer sales**

# Step 3. Allocate to branches

*Inside sales and customer service only.* Divide total salesperson capacity between branches — the
source's example splits $100,000 as $70,000 Houston and $30,000 Austin. **Analyse sales data
regularly to decide the split** rather than holding it fixed.

# Step 4. Inbound cost-per-lead

1. Enter the number of sales expected from **inbound leads**.
2. Enter the expected **cost-per-lead** — this drives the department's total marketing cost.
3. Repeat for **Lead Source #2**, or leave it blank.
4. **Unallocated sales fall into Lead Source #3.** If sales were over-allocated to source #1 or #2,
   #3 shows a **negative number highlighted in red** — adjust the other sources until it balances.
