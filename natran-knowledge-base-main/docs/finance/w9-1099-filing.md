1099s are an **annual** requirement. Both deadlines fall on **January 31**:

- **Recipient copy** — must reach the recipient by January 31
- **IRS filing** — must be filed by January 31, and filing electronically does not extend it

The work that makes January easy happens all year: steps 1 to 3 are continuous, steps 4 to 8 are the
year-end run.

# 1. Collect W-9 forms

**Request** a W-9 from every independent contractor and vendor — the form is downloadable from the IRS
website. **Store** the completed forms in the EOY W-9 folder.

# 2. Set up vendors in QuickBooks Online

**Expenses** → **Vendors** → **New Vendor**, or open an existing vendor to edit.

Fill in name, email, phone number and address. Then:

- Tick **Track payments for 1099**.
- Enter the vendor's **Tax Identification Number** from the W-9 — an SSN or an EIN.
- **Save.**

# 3. Categorise payments as they happen

When paying a vendor, categorise the payment to an expense account that **qualifies for 1099
reporting** — services, fees and similar. Use **+ New** to create invoices or expenses linked to the
vendor.

# 4. Review vendor payments

**Reports** → run the **Vendor Expenses** report and confirm every payment to a 1099 vendor is
categorised correctly.

# 5. Prepare the 1099s

**Taxes** → **1099s** → **Get started** if not yet set up. Choose the tax year → **Continue**.

Review the list of eligible vendors and confirm the amounts are accurate and correctly categorised,
adjusting where needed.

# 6. Review and file

**Preview 1099s** and check names, addresses and amounts.

Then choose a filing method:

- **E-file** — follow the prompts to submit through QuickBooks.
- **Print** — have the correct 1099 forms loaded.

# 7. Send copies to vendors

Send the appropriate copies to vendors by January 31.

# 8. Keep records

Retain a copy of every filed 1099 and its supporting documents.

**Two standing cautions from the source:** check QuickBooks for changes to the 1099 process, since tax
rules move; and consult a tax professional on specific compliance questions.

> This procedure has vendors' **SSNs or EINs** keyed into QuickBooks and their signed W-9s stored in a
> Drive folder. That is ordinary 1099 practice, but it means **the EOY W-9 folder holds taxpayer
> identification numbers** and its access should be scoped accordingly. No identifiers were copied into
> this bundle.
