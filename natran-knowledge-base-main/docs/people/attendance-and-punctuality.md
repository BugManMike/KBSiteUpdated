# Notification

> **The corpus gives three different notice requirements for the same situation. This needs
> resolving — it is the rule staff are disciplined against.**
>
> | Source | Requirement |
> |---|---|
> | Employee Handbook (this policy) | Notify your supervisor **at least 2 hours before** your scheduled shift |
> | Both PTO policies | Notify management **at least one hour before** your next shift |
> | Time off requests via Gusto | Unplanned time off submitted **no later than the Monday before the next payroll** |
>
> The handbook's 2 hours is the strictest and is the one tied to a defined consequence
> ("improper call off"), so it is the safest to follow. But an employee reading only a PTO policy
> would reasonably believe one hour is enough. See
> [PTO — office hourly](/people/pto-office-hourly.md) and
> [time off requests](/people/time-off-requests.md).

**Late arrival or early departure:** notify your supervisor at least 2 hours before your
scheduled shift. **Failure to do so is deemed an improper call off.**

**Unexpected absence:** same 2-hour rule. **Improper call offs result in disciplinary action up
to and including termination.**

**Technicians** have an additional and different deadline: **when calling out, notify your
supervisor by 2 PM on the day of the absence.** Failure to communicate may mean no work route is
available the following day.

> The technician rule is same-day-by-2-PM, which for an early shift is *after* the shift has
> already been missed. It reads as a route-planning deadline layered on top of the 2-hour rule
> rather than a replacement for it, but the handbook does not say so.

After notifying a supervisor, employees must **also submit the absence through the employee
portal**. If unable to report it themselves, they should arrange for someone to call on their
behalf.

> **Which portal is not stated.** Three systems appear across the corpus — Gusto, uAttend, and
> the EOSG/PrismHR portal. See [attendance event logging](/people/attendance-event-logging.md).

# Expectations

- Arrive ready to start work promptly at the designated start time.
- Regular hours are communicated by your supervisor or the company's scheduling guidelines.
- Adhere to the assigned schedule including start times, break periods and end times.
- **You are responsible for knowing your schedule.**
- Any change to the regular schedule must be approved in advance.
- **Mandatory meetings must be attended unless formally excused in writing** by management.
- Record work hours accurately using the designated timekeeping system, and report discrepancies
  promptly.

# Excused exceptions

Tardies may be exempted from counting as an occurrence for **uncontrollable events** — severe
weather, car trouble, traffic, medical — but this **must be approved by the Human Resources
Director or Branch General Manager**. It is not a self-certified exemption.

# Thresholds and consequences

| Threshold | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Any no-call-no-show **during the 90-day probation period** | Disciplinary action up to and including termination |
| Failure to notify of absence for **three consecutive workdays** | Treated as **voluntary resignation** |
| **Two or more** occurrences of unexcused absence in a **30-day period** | Excessive absenteeism — disciplinary action |
| Failure to report for **three days or more** without notifying a supervisor | **Job abandonment** — voluntary termination of the employment relationship |

> The three-consecutive-day rule and the job abandonment rule are stated separately in the
> handbook and describe the same outcome from the same trigger. Not a conflict, but they are
> redundant.

**Patterns** of unacceptable attendance behaviour will be addressed and may lead to disciplinary
action up to and including termination.

# Medical release

For an absence due to illness of **3 days or more**, a medical release may be required. It must
be presented to the Human Resources Department **before returning to work**.
