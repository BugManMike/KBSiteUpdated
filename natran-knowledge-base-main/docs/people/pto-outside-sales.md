Outside sales has its own PTO policy. It is **largely identical** to
[PTO — office hourly](/people/pto-office-hourly.md) — same eligibility, same 90-day wait, same
40-hour carryover, same 4-hour increments, same draw-ahead, same cash-out and forfeiture rules.

**Four things differ**, and they are the reason this is a separate document.

# 1. A different accrual table — with a missing tier

| Years of service | Bi-weekly hours | Annual hours | Annual days |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–24 months | 2.4615 | 64 | 8 |
| 3–4 years | 3.0769 | 80 | 10 |
| 5+ years | 4 | 104 | 13 |

> **There is no tier covering 2 to 3 years of service.** The first band ends at 24 months and the
> next begins at 3 years. An outside sales rep in their third year falls in a gap the policy does
> not address. Compare the office hourly table, which has a distinct **2 years** band. **This
> looks like the 2-year row was dropped when the table was adapted.** HR to confirm what a rep
> between 24 and 36 months accrues.

Outside sales starts **higher** than office hourly (8 days vs 7) but ends **lower** (13 days vs
15).

# 2. No flex days

The office hourly policy grants **five unpaid flex days**. This policy's table has no flex day
column and the accompanying paragraph is absent.

> The flex-day paragraph *is* still present in this document's body text — it describes five
> unpaid flex days for sickness, bereavement and extra holiday time. **Only the table omits
> them.** So the document contradicts itself: the prose grants flex days, the table does not
> record them. HR to confirm whether outside sales gets five flex days.

# 3. A different busy season

**March – May, or October – December.**

> The office hourly policy and the handbook both say **March – August**. Two different busy
> seasons for two groups is plausible — outside sales demand may genuinely peak differently from
> office workload — but nothing states it is deliberate, and the handbook's blanket
> March–August is written as company-wide.

# 4. PTO basis includes salary

Where the office policy says PTO is paid at "the employee's normal hourly rate", this says
**"normal hourly rate, salaried or base pay-rate"** — accommodating salaried and commissioned
reps. Overtime, commissions and bonuses remain excluded.

This policy also adds a clause the other lacks: the company **retains the right to adjust, amend,
revoke or alter the policy at any time**, citing economic conditions, natural calamities and war.

# The signature block is wrong

> The acknowledgment reads *"I understand and agree to this **Office Employee** Time Off
> Policy."* This is the outside sales policy. **The signature block was copied from the office
> hourly document and never retitled** — the same lineage that produced the missing accrual tier
> and the flex-day mismatch. A signed copy of this document therefore acknowledges a policy by
> the wrong name. HR and whoever handles employment paperwork should decide whether that matters.
