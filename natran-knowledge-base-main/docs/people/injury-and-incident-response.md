**HR Department: 832-404-2235.** Call it in every case below.

# Immediate response

**Severe or life-threatening injuries:**

1. **Call 911.**
2. Call HR at **832-404-2235**.
3. Complete the **incident Google Form** within **24 hours** of the incident.

**Everything else:**

1. Call HR at **832-404-2235**.
2. Complete the **incident Google Form** within **24 hours**.
3. If the employee **waives treatment**, they must **sign a waiver**. If they want treatment, they
   can attend a **CareNow** clinic.

> **Report every injury, no matter how small.** The handbook adds that failure to report an
> accident which later develops into a "lost time accident" may make workers' compensation
> benefits difficult to obtain.

## Two reporting mechanisms, and a real difference on drug testing

> The handbook and the safety document describe this differently:
>
> | | Safety document | Employee Handbook |
> |---|---|---|
> | The report | **Incident Google Form**, within 24 hours | **"Employee First Report of Injury"**, completed by the **supervisor**, submitted to HR within 24 hours |
> | Who reports | Not specified | Report to your supervisor immediately; if unavailable, contact HR directly |
> | Drug testing | *"Yes, if the employee receives treatment they will be drug tested"* | **"All work-related injuries will result in a mandatory drug screen"** |
>
> **The drug testing difference is material.** The handbook makes a screen mandatory for every
> work-related injury regardless of treatment; the safety document conditions it on treatment
> being received. An employee who declines treatment would be tested under one document and not
> the other. Since the handbook is the signed instrument, its rule is more likely enforceable —
> but the safety document is what a manager at the scene will read. **HR to reconcile.**
>
> Whether the "Employee First Report of Injury" and the incident Google Form are the same artifact
> under two names, or two separate submissions, is also unclear.

# Treatment

**CareNow** clinics, in both Houston and Austin. Location lists and treatment authorization forms
for each city are held as PDFs in the wiki's Safety Documents folder.

> **The Employee Agreement describes a different treatment path.** Its Workers Compensation
> section says the employee **must choose a treating doctor from the insurance network**, or ask
> their primary care physician to serve as treating doctor and notify **Texas Mutual at
> (844) 867-2338**. It warns they **may have to pay the bill** if they get care from a non-network
> provider without approval. Whether CareNow is in-network is not stated anywhere. **This is worth
> resolving before someone is sent to a clinic that does not get paid.** See
> [employee agreement map](/people/employee-agreement-map.md).

**Emergency care** may be obtained anywhere, per the agreement.

# After the accident

1. **The doctor determines** whether the employee returns without restrictions or is released for
   **light duty**. HR and the Branch Manager review the restrictions **and any medications** to
   assess risk. Consult **Employer One-Source Group** for guidance.
2. The employee **signs a light duty document** detailing job duties, working hours and rate of
   pay.
3. The employee **attends all** doctor and rehabilitation appointments as required.
4. The employee obtains a **release to return to work without restrictions**.

> **Missing an appointment has consequences:** denial of claim, denial of any lost-wage claims,
> and possible disciplinary action.

# Questions that come up

**Can an employee refuse treatment?** Yes, but they must sign a waiver. **They cannot be forced
to get treatment** — but a doctor's release can be required before they return to work.

**Can they change their mind later?** Yes. The right to treatment survives an initial refusal and
a signed waiver, **though the window may be limited.**

**Suspected drugs or alcohol at the time?** Management can send them to a clinic for drug testing
even if they refuse treatment. Nova testing locations are used.

**Injured outside work?** **Not covered.** But if management believes the injury puts them at
risk, a doctor's release can be required before they return.

**Can the company get medical records?** Yes, for work-related injuries. **The employee signs a
release when hired**, so this is not a HIPAA violation.

**Will lost wages be paid?** **Generally no.** The employee is assigned light duty per the
doctor's recommendation. Only if they are **not released for light duty, or light duty is not
available**, may they be eligible — paid through workers' compensation insurance at **a rate
lower than normal pay**, and **excluding the first week** after injury.

**Can they use PTO for that first week?** They **may** be eligible to use accrued PTO. Consult
Employer One-Source Group.
