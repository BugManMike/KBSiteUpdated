The company recognises **six holidays** each year and closes on them.

- New Year's Day
- Memorial Day
- Independence Day
- Labor Day
- Thanksgiving Day
- Christmas Day

If a holiday falls on a weekend it is observed on **either the preceding or following day**, at
management's determination, communicated to eligible employees.

# Who gets holiday pay

Eligible **after the initial 90 days of employment**:

- **Exempt (salaried)** employees
- **Hourly office staff (full-time)**

> **"Other employee categories are not eligible for this pay."** Read against
> [employment classification](/people/employment-classification.md), that excludes part-time
> staff, temporary staff — and **technicians**, who are neither exempt nor office staff. Since
> technicians are the largest group and the company closes on these days, this is the line most
> worth confirming with HR. It is stated plainly, so it appears deliberate rather than an
> oversight, but it is not called out as a deliberate exclusion anywhere.

The company **reserves the right to rescind this policy at any time.**

# Conditions

- **Holiday pay does not count as time worked for overtime calculations.**
- Computed at the employee's **base rate of pay**. **Salary only — no commission or bonuses.**
- An employee **scheduled to work a holiday** is paid their regular rate **plus 8 hours of holiday
  pay**.
- **Not paid** to employees on any type of unpaid leave.
- A holiday falling inside approved scheduled vacation is **recorded as holiday pay**, not PTO.
- **The attendance condition:** employees must be present **on their last scheduled day before
  the holiday and the first scheduled day after it** to receive holiday pay.
  - If absent on either because of illness or injury, the company **may require verification**
    before approving holiday pay.

> The last condition is the one that catches people. Taking the day before Thanksgiving off sick,
> without verification, can forfeit the holiday pay itself.

# Religious observances

Employees needing time off for religious practices or holidays not on the company list should
speak with their supervisor. Options include using accrued **PTO** or taking an **unpaid day**.
The company states a commitment to making reasonable accommodations.
