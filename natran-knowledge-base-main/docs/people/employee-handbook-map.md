**The Google Doc is authoritative.** The handbook is a signed instrument — it ends with an
*Employee Handbook Acknowledgment and Receipt* and a separate *Acknowledgment and Receipt of
Harassment Policy*, both of which employees sign. Nothing in this bundle supersedes it.

This document exists because the handbook is ~50 policies in one 110 KB file with no working
index, so nobody can find which policy governs what.

# Why most of it was not extracted

A handbook's authority comes from being the document employees signed. Extracting all fifty
sections into separate concept files would create a **second copy of a signed legal document that
can drift from the original** — which is the exact failure mode `DECISIONS.md` OQ-005 is about,
applied to a document with legal consequences.

**So the extraction was selective.** Nine sections were pulled out because operational documents
elsewhere in the corpus depend on them, or because staff act on them daily. The rest are mapped
here and left in place.

> **This is a judgment call, not a rule from the brief.** If you would rather have the whole
> handbook shredded into concepts, say so and it can be done — the argument against is divergence
> risk on a signed document, not effort.

# Overlaps with the Employee Agreement

**Several policies exist in both signed documents.** The Uniform Policy and Dress Code appear in
the handbook *and* as section 2.6 of the Employee Agreement, in near-identical wording. Cell phone
and device rules appear in both. See
[employee agreement map](/people/employee-agreement-map.md).

> Two signed documents carrying the same policy can drift independently, and an employee has signed
> both. Worth consolidating — or at least deciding which one wins.

# Structural notes

The handbook opens with a **Table of Contents** whose entries do not match the body's section
order, and the ToC lists nine groupings while the body runs to about fifty headings. The
`Mandatory Wage Deductions Policy` heading carries stray asterisks, and `Medical Release/Dr 's
Notes` has a spurious space. Cosmetic, but they indicate the document has been edited in place
over time rather than reissued.
